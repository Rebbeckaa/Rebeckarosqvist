November 2025 · WCAG 2.1 Redesign · UX / Accessibility

underbara unge

An accessibility-first redesign of an app that helps parents capture their child's everyday moments.

Overview

Underbara Unge is a mobile application concept designed to help parents document their child's development. This redesign focused on improving usability, clarity, and accessibility according to WCAG 2.1 AA standards — creating a more intuitive user flow while ensuring inclusive design for a broader range of users.

The problem

The original design presented issues with low contrast, unclear navigation, and insufficient accessibility considerations. This risked increasing cognitive load and reducing usability for a broader audience.

Before

Registration

Password requirements were hidden and only revealed one at a time, causing frustration before users even got started.

Users had to guess the password rules, leading to repeated failed attempts.

After

Registration

All requirements are shown upfront with real-time validation — users know exactly what's needed before they type.

Clear requirements and instant feedback make sign-up smooth and frustration-free.

My role

This was a collaborative academic project developed in a team setting. I contributed throughout the design process, including user flows and high-fidelity prototyping in Figma.

My primary responsibility was ensuring accessibility compliance according to WCAG 2.1 AA — including contrast adjustments, improved form structure, and clearer interactive elements.

Research

We developed a persona and mapped the user journey to identify friction points and accessibility gaps in the current experience, then used those insights to prioritise the redesign.

Key learnings

This project deepened my understanding of accessibility as a design responsibility rather than a checklist. I learned how small decisions — contrast ratios, form structure, feedback states — significantly impact usability.

It strengthened my ability to evaluate interfaces critically and design with inclusivity as a core principle.
